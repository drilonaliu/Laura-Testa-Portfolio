How I Work

Understanding isn't always enough to change things.

Many people arrive already insightful, able to describe their patterns clearly, and still find nothing shifts. That's what I find most compelling about this work: understanding something doesn't always free us from it. What helps is rarely more explanation. Real change tends to come through a steady, trusting relationship, one solid enough that patterns you've carried for a long time finally have room to shift. I work integratively, at your pace, following what's actually in front of us rather than a fixed method.

About

A practice built on research and lived experience

My route into this work wasn't a straight line. I studied psychology across two countries before completing an MSc, and my research, published in the Journal of Clinical Child Psychology and Psychiatry, examined ambiguous loss in the mothers of the missing in Kosovo.

I later directed and produced Waiting, a documentary on ambiguous loss that won Best National Film at Dokufest. Before that, I spent years working in war and post-war environments, in the field with international human rights organisations and press during the conflicts in Kosovo, southern Serbia and Macedonia. That work shaped my capacity to stay steady with distress that doesn't resolve easily or quickly.

I've also worked closely alongside speech and language therapists, including direct experience of aphasia and the ways it can disrupt communication. It's given me a particular patience for moments when words aren't enough, and for finding other ways for someone to be understood.

I also bring a strong professional interest in the third culture kid experience: the particular kind of identity that forms when "where are you from" never has a simple answer.

Approach & Specialisms

Grounded, integrative, and led by what you actually need.

Ambiguous loss & grief

For losses without a clear ending, such as missing loved ones, estrangement, dementia or chronic illness, where grief has nowhere obvious to land.

Trauma & its aftermath

Working with the lasting effects of war, displacement or single-incident trauma, at a pace your nervous system can actually tolerate.

Third culture kids & cross-cultural identity

For anyone who grew up across countries and cultures, working through questions of belonging, rootlessness, and building an identity that isn't tied to one place.

When words are hard to find

For people navigating communication difficulties, such as aphasia, neurological change, or simply the sense that language isn't enough, in themselves or someone they love.

Frequently Asked Questions

A few things people usually want to know

What kinds of things do people bring to you?

All sorts. Some arrive already knowing exactly what they want to work on, and others just know that something feels off.

How long does therapy usually take?

It depends on what you're working through, and we'll decide this together. I work in two ways:

Short-term, focused work, typically around 12 weeks. Useful for a specific issue or a clearer, time-limited piece of work.

Open-ended, longer-term work, without a fixed end date. Better suited to deeper or more longstanding patterns. We'll agree a finishing point together when the time feels right.

Is everything I say confidential?

All discussions in counselling and therapy are confidential. There are, of course, some legal exceptions and limitations to this, which exist to help keep you safe, and these will be explained clearly at your first meeting.

All client records are protected under the General Data Protection Regulation and the Data Protection Act, and no client information is shared with any third party. Any exceptions to this would occur only where necessary to protect the safety of clients or others.

If you ask me not to contact you on a particular number or at a particular address, I won't.

What's your cancellation policy?

Your session time is held for you alone, so if you're unable to attend and don't give enough notice, the full session fee still applies. If I cancel a session you will not be charged. Any planned breaks, such as holidays, will always be agreed between us in advance.

How do I know if you're the right fit?

Before we meet, I offer a free 10-minute phone call to get a sense of what's bringing you to therapy and to check our diaries align. This step isn't compulsory. Fit is really something we explore properly in the first session itself. If it doesn't feel right, there's no pressure to continue.
